    How the radar works

    The value model

    A row's implied launch value comes from one curve, fitted fresh on every snapshot from the protocols whose tokens already trade: log10(market cap) regressed on log10(TVL). The curve is the market's own size curve — a dollar of TVL in a giant protocol is worth fewer dollars of market cap than a dollar in a small one, and the slope says by how much. The same function scores every protocol, and no name is ever hardcoded.

    Two dampers order near-ties. Growth: 1 + 0.30 × the 7-day TVL change, clamped to −50%..+100%, so a hot week lifts a row by at most 30%. Maturity: 0.70 + 0.30 × age/1095 days, so a protocol that has run three years without a token is worth at most 30% more than a fresh listing. The curve does the ranking; the dampers only order near-ties.

    How the fit is tested

    Two out-of-sample checks run on the same traded set, and their numbers are printed on the board. A time split fits on protocols listed before 2025-01-01 and tests on the ones listed after; a 5-fold cross-validation over a seeded shuffle does the same five times over. Each reports the median and mean error as a factor: ×3.25 means the typical held-out market cap lands within a factor of 3.25 of the curve. The old per-category median multiple scored ×4.82 on the same time split. The error is still wide, and the board says so: an implied value is a model claim, never a price.

    What the board refuses to rank

    An exchange, a bridge, a versioned pool or a wrapped or staked product of an asset that already trades can never launch a token of its own, so ranking one first would be the radar finding nothing. The watchlist is the floor, never the ceiling: all of its slugs rank, plus every other pre-token protocol the rule allows, whatever its TVL — the board's {{EARLY_SIZE}} early rows are the radar, the watchlist is only where it starts. A protocol is called pre-token only because its record carries no token: the record is not proof. USDD is a stablecoin whose token is its product; it ranks because the watchlist requires it and its record carries no token — treat its row as the curve's reading, not a launch prediction.

    The farm economics

    Every plan is priced in dollars: gas for the setup and one weekly transaction across the hold, plus the opportunity cost of the capital at 5% a year. The expected value assumes a 5% farmer allocation of the launch, split among 2,000 farmers; both numbers are stated on every row, and the arithmetic is shown, so a reader who disagrees can redo it. Net is expected value minus plan cost: FARM when it clears $200, THIN when it clears $100, SKIP when it clears neither. Capital follows the protocol's TVL ($250 above $1B, $150 above $100M, $100 above $10M, $50 below), the hold follows its age (60 days under six months, 45 under a year, 30 beyond), and gas follows its main chain (Ethereum $8 a transaction, cheap chains $0.50, the rest $2).

    What this is not

    The bounty does not predict whether or when a token is issued. A verdict is a budgeting aid for a farmer, and a row's implied value is what the market's own size curve would pay for a protocol of that size — nothing more.
